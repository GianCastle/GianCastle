Without a doubt, social media plays a fundamental, even unnecessary, role in our lives. At least, if you believe the statistics, it's a big part of our lives. Let me show you a few of them

- There are 3.4 billion active social media users. Who, on average, have 7.6 social media accounts and spend 116 minutes a day on social media.
- 91% of retail brands use two or more social media channels and 81% of all small and medium-sized businesses use some kind of social platform.

The numbers keep growing and they may not stop for a good while. Look at this, social media users grew to 320 million between September 2017 and October 2018. Although on the other hand, according to an analysis done in mid-April, there was a certain decline in the quintessential social networks (Facebook, Twitter and Instagram) as a result of the pandemic caused by Covid-19.

- **Facebook**: Outgoing posts decreased by an average of 1.8 posts per day
- **Instagram**: Outgoing posts decreased by 1.9 posts per day
- **Twitter**: Outgoing messages decreased by 2.3 messages per day

Social media has a significant impact on the way we live our lives. And, according to a recent article from The Washington Post, our friends' posts also push us to buy and consume more and more.

There are a number of factors (both internal and external) that push us to consume in the society we live in. Social media is certainly one of the main causes and it's worth pointing out why.

This is how I see social media influencing us to buy:

- **Personal spending is more visible**. Let's see... "Humans are social creatures, and we have a tendency to evaluate our own standing in life in relation to how our friends and neighbors and everyone around us are doing. We want to keep up with the Joneses, and stay ahead of the Smiths. That's why, when we see other people spending money, we have a tendency to think that we can - or should - spend too." There's no doubt this is true! Seeing others spend money on things (houses, food, vacations) nudges us, subtly motivates us to do the same.
- **More (and better targeted) advertising**. Every media platform offers the opportunity to advertise (television, newspaper, radio, magazines, etc.) and each one provides some level of targeting (a reader of Shape Magazine will see different ads than a reader of National Geographic). But no platform in history has provided the opportunity for targeted advertising like social networks. As Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, Pinterest, YouTube, (even Amazon) collect more and more personal data about us, the ads we see become more and more relevant as companies use that data to choose who sees their ads.
- **The great convenience of buying from your device.** Advertising on social media is also more effective because the opportunity to buy is immediate. In the old days, if you saw an ad on television, you had to remember it for the next time you went to the store to buy it; not to mention the home shopping ads. This inconvenience reduced the effectiveness of those ads. But nowadays, the friction has been removed. We see an ad on social media and we're immediately offered the chance to buy the item in a matter of seconds with just a few finger movements .
- **Eating and getting fat on Likes**. It's not only what we see of others on social media that influences us to buy, we often buy because of what we want others to see of us. It's all too easy to fall into the trap of chasing and hunting Likes and followers on social media - the creators reward us for it. Nice things, trendy products, fancy destinations, and quality food and drink play well on social media. Most of us know it, and we spend more than we should, just to impress people on social media. But there are better ways to impress others.
- **The "movie" we share online**. What we share on social media are fictional versions of ourselves. Think about it. Nobody posts photos of their bad hair day on Social media or shares their deepest struggles and problems. Instead, we present others with a mostly made-up version of ourselves. These fictional versions of people we run into for two hours every day online paint an unrealistic picture of what life is like. When we start comparing our lives to theirs, we start chasing an idealized life, often believing that if we spend more, we can reach it.
- **The negative emotions caused by Social media**. The studies have been done and the research is underway. social media doesn't, in general, make us feel better about ourselves. For many of the reasons mentioned, social media users tend to report higher levels of depression, loneliness and lower self-esteem. These emotions of isolation and discontent often result in retail therapy or in trying to overcome our discontent with consumption-based purchases.
- **Influencers and their influence**. It's redundant, but let me explain... It doesn't take long for a new company or product to get recognized by making a splash on social media. Companies and brands that used to spend time and money and goodwill building a brand can now pull it off in a little while and a few DMs. Influencers are available for hire and can almost immediately start a conversation about a new trend. But just because someone paid an influencer to have their picture taken with their new bag or shoe line doesn't mean it's something necessary in our life today, or ever.

Social media, it seems to me, is here to stay. And the platforms being built and expanded offer wonderful opportunities to enrich our lives but at the same time they destroy and create patterns that have dehumanized our society.

But just like the invention of fire, it's going to take some time for society to figure out how to use it for good and not for evil.
