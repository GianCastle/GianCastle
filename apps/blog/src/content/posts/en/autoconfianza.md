Each of us is born with an inner resource that, had it been nurtured and cultivated by our parents or caregivers at an early age, would have made it easier for us to thrive in life with self-love.

This resource is self-trust. Think about it: every infant knows when they're hungry and tired. Every small child knows what they like to eat and who they want to socialize with. As children, we grow up knowing ourselves; our rhythms, tastes and preferences. But since the "grown-ups" around us always told us that they knew better, that things are done the way they understand them, we believed them and our intrinsic trust in ourselves got buried.

Eventually, we conditioned ourselves to seek the approval and guidance of others instead of trusting our own. As a result, many of us suffer from the symptoms of insecurity: paralyzing indecision, a constant desire for approval, perfectionism and fear of failure.

Self-trust doesn't only get buried under the belief that everyone else knows better than we do; it's also weakened by our constant desire to receive external validation. Self-trust sits like a precious stone at the bottom of the well of our being. When our well is filled with our own waters, when you know yourself and trust yourself, it will begin to rise to the surface, you'll begin to take yourself into account, you'll begin to love yourself..

In other words, knowing yourself + loving yourself = trusting yourself.

We learn from the very beginning that if we do things "right", we'll receive the smiles of the people we're attached to... oh, how those smiles feel like sunlight to any individual! When we seek validation this way, it's like walking around holding a bottomless bucket, waiting for it to fill up with the moisture in the air. Although we may experience happiness or calm when someone gives us their approval, these feelings actually last a very, very short time.

That's why the bucket has no bottom: we could spend the rest of our lives climbing a corporate, educational, creative or social ladder. We could outwardly achieve everything we ever thought would bring happiness. And yet, we could still be left without the experience of true and sustainable fulfillment.

Healthy self-trust is like having an internal GPS system: you know where to go next, you trust your decisions both big and small, and you're willing to take risks. You're not afraid of failure or of making mistakes because your sense of self isn't derived externally. As someone I follow on social media says, *you become a man, you become a person of action.*

Just as we **don't** look at a newborn and say: "I'll love you if you attend Harvard Medical School and marry a beautiful partner and have two healthy kids and make a lot of dough", we shouldn't make the notion of loving ourselves conditional on external factors.

We are worthy because we exist. Your self-doubt and perfectionism may be getting in the way of that potential manifesting, but it's there, deep down, like an effervescent river finding its way from its inner depths out into the world.

Is it possible to learn to trust yourself when you've abdicated your trust in yourself your whole life? 
Without a doubt. At the end of the day, trusting ourselves is one of our birthrights; it was ours from the beginning and it still lives inside us, sitting intact like a gleaming crystal.

As in all areas of personal growth, the path of healing requires time, patience and above all, **commitment**. Change doesn't happen in thirty days or in three simple steps, but I've personally followed these rules (which are simple) that have helped me redirect my attention from the external to the internal, thus beginning the process of restoring or improving my trust:

1. **Start and end each day by turning inward**
This means that instead of reaching for your phone first thing in the morning, or having it be the last thing you do at the end of the day, reach for yourself instead; do something you like, read some inspiring book, exercise, do some recreational activity. Take some time for yourself, instead of giving it to third parties.

2. **Go on the Social Media diet**
If you struggle with worrying about what others think, Facebook is guaranteed to amplify that struggle. It's almost impossible to go on Instagram, Facebook, or look at a Whatsapp status without comparing yourself to others in some way. If Facebook or that blessed messaging app is a way of communicating with your friends, try picking up the phone instead; if not, like the good Dominican I am... ***Just drop it***

Experiment with these suggestions and see how you feel. I have yet to work with someone who didn't notice a difference in their levels of self-trust and peace when they implemented these slight changes and learned to channel their precious time and energy inward in a healthier way.
