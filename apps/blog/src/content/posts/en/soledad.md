We're fearful creatures, and that's a good thing. Fear lights the flame of survival. Fear marks the limits clearly. Fear reminds you of, and helps you value, the very short time we have. Of course, any fear that turns pathological and triggers an outsized physiological response doesn't help at all.

When fear is disproportionate and gets anticipated long before the event happens, without knowing for sure whether it will happen, we'd be entering the territory of clinical anxiety. However, of all those fears, few feel as deeply suffocating as the fear of feeling lonely.

Being alone is a mere coincidence in time and space and means nothing if it isn't accompanied by the feeling of loneliness, which means that feeling lonely goes further, it goes to that dangerous place of feeling lonely even if you're surrounded by people. Most people have felt it, because throughout life there are many situations that can trigger that feeling of loneliness, something like a death, a divorce… getting fired. Even subtler things like moving house could set it off, but unless it's psychologically very shocking, people end up getting over them.

Here I want to focus on what happens when you don't get over it, when you enter the vicious circle; when instead of taking off the chains, you tighten them even more, when you come to the realization that deep down we're all alone and you convince yourself of it… it's like The Problem of Other Minds; deep down you can only be sure of your own mind, and that's as far as it goes. Nobody can get inside your head, everyone else is unreal, they have shape because you give it to them. Only you exist and only you can know it. Philosophy, sure, but if you internalize that thought very deeply, the loneliness it makes you feel is downright oppressive because…

***The human being's greatest fear has never been death, but being forgotten.***

If situations like a death, a divorce, a separation and getting fired tend to unleash that feeling of loneliness, it's because they share the feeling of being forgotten. If you get fired, you feel useless in front of one that no longer needs you and seems to want to forget about you; a divorce hurts because that person who was everything to you now only wants to forget you, and death is the ultimate expression of forgetting and being forgotten… thinking that a hundred years from now absolutely nobody will remember you; that's when the real dread begins. That's why loneliness is so terrible, it's like being dead while alive, it's having the worst of both options; being forgotten and, on top of that, being aware of it, that is the human being's greatest fear, and close to 20% of the world's population (and rising) is feeling it, because new technologies and social networks don't offer real contact, it can't be replaced, and the danger is believing that they do, because gradually less time gets invested in really interacting… the kind where you don't sit staring at a screen to see if the double check has turned blue in some Damned Messaging App; not even a video call would give the same feeling.

While I'm on this subject, I've seen that new technologies get demonized to an abysmal degree, that we accuse them of being the cause of the key factor behind this evil, but that's seeing only one side of the coin, because it also helps. This doesn't get solved the way someone I heard on some Podcasts proposed… by getting rid of the thing causing the problem, in this case, all technology. Sure, to some extent one group will improve, but it'll harm the other.

The way I see it, the issue has to be approached as if it were the issue of drugs… educate well, let people know it doesn't replace a real interaction, and hope the person on the receiving end is responsible enough to know how to handle that information, rather than taking the easy road of scaring people. Just like in the real world, the digital one also distinguishes quality relationships. So the problem isn't so much simply using it, but how often, that it doesn't become obsessive, and the quality… that it feels like a complement and not a substitute. One group that benefits is the elderly; for them the internet is as therapeutic as having a pet. Knowing how to handle it, they won't end up feeling completely isolated, they'll still feel connected (literally) to the world. True, it'll never be the same, but it's far and away better than being stuck in their own house, watching time go by, and even the much-demonized video games can bring some relief, because you get a bastardized version of what it is to be part of a group. Again, it shouldn't replace anything, but even so it helps them keep feeling that necessary connection to the world.

I'd love to see a future where games are used therapeutically in serious cases of addiction, instead of disowning them as the cause of all evils, and likewise social networks, to stop seeing them as a problem and start thinking about how they can help. In short, stop crapping on technology, look for the benefits and boost them.

I consider loneliness an epidemic… elderly people or those who have difficulty communicating are always the ones who suffer the most, but this epidemic has been spreading to everyone, regardless of age, sex, culture and belief. It's incredible to see how it's the youngest, us, who feel lonelier than the elderly themselves; it's as if the world were folding in on itself… there's no clear enemy. When you compare it to the opioid epidemic the United States has been suffering, it's not the same. In that epidemic the enemy has a shape, this one is invisible, it's trying to get out of a room that has no doors or windows. Your doctor normally won't ask you whether you have healthy social relationships, even though that also affects your health; and even if he did ask, he can't prescribe you crap. Sure, you can do therapy, but let's remember that therapy is a voluntary process, and let's also remember that you're locked in a room you neither want to nor can leave… you start to feel that exposing yourself to others makes you vulnerable and you don't want to feel that way. The idea of what relating to someone else is starts to become distorted, to become ambiguous, always guarding against the idea of being able to connect deeply with someone because you believe it leaves you defenseless.

All of that above looks like a method of self-preservation. But in the long run, social relationships come to be perceived as artificial or, at best, superficial.

Let's look at it this way… if someone always shows their best face, always seems to be happy, has no moments of weakness, it's a warning that they're not being totally sincere. They've turned their life into something like Instagram; always showing their best face. But as good as it may look, they're treating their relationships artificially, and by this I mean they don't really connect, and without that blessed connection, you start to feel lonely.

>
Yes, it seems like just a feeling, feeling lonely. But it has enough power to destroy from within. Not long ago the European Society of Cardiology presented a study called Loneliness Is Bad For The Heart, which shows once again that this feeling deteriorates the immune system and favors premature death as we adopt a harmful lifestyle. With this life, we tend to drink more alcohol, sleeping becomes a Bad Habit, stress becomes widespread and you simply distrust without shame or judgment; on top of which, over time, anxiety and depression are guaranteed. Curiously (although the difference with men isn't much) women bear loneliness less well, they're even more likely to suffer any of the problems I mentioned above, but as I said that difference is very small, almost nil.

That fear hurts the human race especially because we're a species that, for better or worse, needs validation, needs to feel connected in some way. That feedback shapes us, helps us fit into a world that, however you look at it… makes no sense. But as I already said… it doesn't need to, as long as you give it one. That connection helps create bonds, feel that you can trust someone, feel loved, because otherwise you'll feel abandoned, trying to force yourself to fit… like that toddler banging a square puzzle piece that clearly doesn't fit into a triangular shape.

When it happens and the feeling of loneliness intensifies, the message it sends is clear; you feel set apart or rejected by your own species. I repeat that this rejection doesn't need to happen explicitly, I'm talking about sensations, about emotions; if something has made them believe it, they'll feel it so vividly that they'll respond with resentment. A spiral of emotions will begin, of shame, disgust, hatred, lack of confidence, contempt… and if they continue and accept their loneliness, it will create or worsen the social anxiety that put them in this situation. It's a two-way thing, something like depression, although it's more common to hear that depression and anxiety increase the feeling of loneliness because of the tendency to isolate. These can also be created by the feeling of loneliness.  Social relationships start to look more threatening, they're no longer seen as interactions you can enjoy…. They get overanalyzed until they're distressing, more attention is paid to the negative signals than the positive ones, they're seen as challenges… hence the fear. And the anticipation of that fear, that is, anxiety, will tell them it's better to be alone. This way the cycle will make its first turn; the more turns it makes, the harder it will be to get out and the more their mental and physical health will worsen, they start finding more justifications to isolate themselves, and doing so hurts them even more… constantly exhausted, fed up, tired of being tired. To be clear, we don't all need the same levels of social interaction to keep from feeling lonely. There are those who are more used to isolation, are more solitary and last longer before feeling the need to see another human being. However, there's also the other side… those for whom a day without leaving the house is a crime. They have such a need for human contact that they could maintain, and do maintain, clearly harmful relationships… just so as not to be alone. The greater the need to interact with other people, the more frequently and on more occasions one will feel lonely.

**Loneliness is painful, it makes even dying not something to fear, because more than being scared by the idea of it happening… it's it happening without anyone noticing.**
**But… why?**

Some blame technology, but it doesn't bear all the responsibility… it can cause a bit of isolation, but as I already said, the tool is useful, abusing it isn't… others say that our society favors individualism more than ever, and although being alone isn't synonymous with feeling lonely, it's also undeniable that the individualist tends to isolate… .There are those who argue that it's only collateral damage from another, even bigger problem, that with an increase in mental illnesses like depression and anxiety it's normal for the feeling of loneliness to grow too. Once again… people who tend to isolate themselves from the world. Looking at all the ideas, it's curious to find that they share the point that, whatever it is that causes it, we have more and more of a need to pull away from the world, or what amounts to the same thing, to believe we don't need anyone, because we believe it protects us, because we believe it's a trait that's valued positively… because it's the profile a successful entrepreneur would have, and by success I mean having money (who doesn't want to have money?), only that turning it into a goal is the biggest mistake you can make… It makes you feel lonely, empty because there's no meaning; anxious because if the purpose is to make money the goal always moves and you'll never reach it. Don't get me wrong, money is useful; everything being by and for making money isn't the healthiest mindset you can have. I read a comment that said they'd like to have enough money to feel empty inside. At first it seems like a witty comment mocking the classic “money doesn't make you happy”; but deep down it's stupid… because he knows it, or senses it rather, but still wants to chase the idea that will make him unhappy, just because deep down he believes it isn't true, or believes it's true but doesn't care because he wants more money than he could spend; buying things he doesn't need and that won't make him feel better… as a life plan, so-so. But it's not his fault for thinking that way, that's how they want you to think, they want you to wish to win the lottery, but not to be happy. You can notice it if instead of money you talk about work.

**Do you remember what happens when a job is lost? Have you felt it?**
You'll feel that the world is ending, that everything is completely over. You get distressed, you feel there's no solution, everything revolves around money. It helps you live, because for better or worse, without getting into this almost philosophical territory, that's how our civilization is built, but it doesn't by itself give your life meaning. It's important that you know nobody will do this for you, you choose what meaning you want to give to life, and you should, because… if you don't, the world will empty you out until there's nothing left of you.

Being alone was the most dangerous thing there was millions of years ago, it made you vulnerable and anything could kill you, nobody protected you, you felt unsafe; the last thing they wanted was to be left alone, because they saw that those who were didn't last long. We still carry that, even though it's no longer needed from an objective point of view; because it keeps getting easier to be self-sufficient and independent, there are those who settle into that lifestyle and don't need to be physically with people to feel they're still connected to the world, that they're part of a group, but… there are also those who for some reason can't feel that connection even when surrounded by a crowd.

***Imagine living through the greatest fear anyone can experience, and that they can't see it and can't understand it, because the reality is that… you're alone facing the problem of feeling lonely.***
