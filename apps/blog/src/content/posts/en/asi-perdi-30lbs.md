I had set myself the goal to  _Revive My Muscles_. I really wanted to see myself vascular again. However, between work stuff, big mistakes, a temporary depression, anxiety and a terrible urge to procrastinate, the only thing I managed to do was kill my muscles even more, gaining roughly 22 pounds. This means that, already overweight to begin with (some 202 pounds), I gained some 22 more, for a lovely total of **224**.

This brought several consequences that affected me both emotionally and physically:
- Walking with any intensity was hard for me, and I had to pause for a moment to relax the hypertrophy it created in my calves.
- I couldn't do the typical squat with 45 pounds on each side on a free bar.
- My scapular retractions were atrophied. I suffered from lower back pain from sitting for prolonged periods.
- I used to do an estimated 25 pull-ups in a single go. With my excess weight, 5 were more than enough to have my arms almost exploding from the swelling.
- I completely lost my grip strength. I couldn't hang from a bar for a few seconds.
- I'd get certain stabbing pains in my heart from time to time. An electrocardiogram showed that I had a mild pericarditis (inflammation of the pericardium due to fluid retention)

I couldn't stand the idea of staying in this grim state, so I immediately took matters into my own hands.

##### I changed my approach

### I misplaced the word diet

This may sound a bit confusing since we immediately relate the term to losing fat and weight; and it's true, with a diet you lose fat and weight. But that's precisely why I recommend **not going on one**, or even using this word. 

When we hear the word diet, our mind immediately relates it to *restriction*, *not eating*, and *limiting yourself*, which makes us see this as something difficult or maybe unreachable; especially in a world as manufactured and processed as today's.

**Do you follow someone on social media who always has an aesthetic physique?**

As a rule, these people remove this word from their vocabulary; they replaced it with some other word like *nutrition*. These people enjoy a healthy lifestyle. When you do something and you love what you do, it's a breeze to keep it up for long periods of time.

If the method you use to lose weight and lower your body fat levels is based on deprivation and the elimination of certain foods, then you probably won't be able to sustain it forever. What I mean is simple: at some point you're going to end up gaining that weight back, or even a lot more; and that's not the right approach. You have to adopt a healthy eating lifestyle and base it around **acceptable** meals. By acceptable I mean that you should eat instinctively (eat what's necessary and no more) and learn to choose your meals.

You can diet, you can have a healthy eating style. However, physical conditioning, the movement of our muscle fibers, is fundamental to achieving a change in our body structure. Which is why I also...

### Set aside the typical gym routines

One of my excuses for not going to the gym last year was that the gym had me **totally bored**. Almost an hour and a half always doing the same thing... pushing and pulling dumbbells, bars with plates. The reality was that, besides being someone who prefers athletic conditioning over aesthetic, I was realizing that I was going to the gym without any focus, objective or short-term goal. I was just going for the sake of going, and that's a serious mistake.

Before going back to the gym I worked on my mindset in such a way that I created a small plan  in which I built a certain dynamism into my workouts. I turned my workouts into upper/lower style routines. These routines are excellent for beginners and for fast physical conditioning. As its name explains, this routine is easy to recognize by the following characteristics:

- Its split and training frequency (one day upper body, the next day legs)
- A big focus on compound exercises (Bench Press, Pull-ups, Squats, Deadlift, etc.)
- Split progressions. Days of heavy loads and low reps; and vice versa. Allowing you to evenly improve Sarcomeric and Sarcoplasmic Hypertrophy (strength and volume)

I don't always go to the gym. I'm more passionate about outdoor exercise. In the mornings I usually run and do calisthenics, and at night I do weight training, or working outside the gym can become my workout for the day. Long story short, **variety** in the way I train became my priority to keep an active focus and make my workouts more fun.
