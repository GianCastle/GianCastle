Caring for someone, loving and being in love are totally different things. Caring is a feeling that drives us to give our affection, fondness and appreciation to a person, to something that can be a place or even a situation.  Loving, however, goes much further than caring... loving is an act, it is being, it is mainly about accepting, without judgments or pretexts. When love is experienced, we are capable of elevating our consciousness. 

Falling in love or being in love, on the other hand, is an obsession that has nothing to do with loving and very rarely with caring. Falling in love implies attachment and illusion, a projection onto some situation, person or thing, where characteristics of an idealized model in the mind of the one suffering the obsession or infatuation are artificially made to match the real object. That is why it is wrongly said that love is blind. Love is not blind, what makes us blind is being in love because we daydream instead of appreciating. Infatuation is most associated with romantic relationships, although we can also fall in love with an object or an idea, that is, become obsessed with an object to which we attribute special characteristics.

Unlike infatuation, when we love we appreciate things, ideas or people just as they are, without idealizing them. It is different from caring, because ideas, things or people are accepted without trying to change or dominate them, they are accepted with their virtues and weaknesses. The one in love, given their obsession, projects their illusion onto the other, making them artificially match the characteristics of someone who only exists in their mind (a prior construction). They tend to exaggerate the attributes they consider positive and justify, deny or ignore those characteristics they overlook but that later, with time, they will consider “flaws”. Infatuation tends to last a short time because the rigor of day-to-day life gradually fades the fog of illusion that keeps the one suffering from it from seeing, and little by little, as the “blindness” passes, they begin to perceive those aspects that were always there but had been overlooked, the ones they never observed or took into account.

 **Frequently, infatuation arises as the impossibility of loving ourselves**, of accepting ourselves, of assuming ourselves as complete and individual beings, and we end up looking in others for what we don't know how to find in ourselves, what we believe we are missing. So, we can say that infatuation is nothing more than a mistaken way of loving ourselves through another person. 

**Why is loving more than a feeling?** 

_Because loving goes beyond caring, it is a perspective in which the other is recognized as a valuable individual in their whole dimension, including the one we are not capable of appreciating._
