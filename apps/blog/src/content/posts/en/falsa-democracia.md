# A democratic system...

It's clear that a democratic political system requires people who are autonomous, people who have a certain level of education both academic and cultural, full access to information and an absolute capacity to express, exchange and promote ideas.

On the other hand, it's considered a minimum requirement within a democratic system that our will as citizens be represented in the creation of a form of state through a system of transparent, free and informed elections. However, those who promote that democratic ideal also endorse the idea that these systems of representation that we ourselves demand must not consist of the simple delegation of sovereignty for operational reasons, but rather must provide for and permanently exercise the constant participation of the represented (us) in the deliberations and decisions.

But... in practice, the exercise of what Popular Sovereignty is gets considered a mode of a democratic system, not its foundation, nor its content. Rooting its reflection in something like Kantian idealism. Some think that the basis of democracy is absolute respect for individual human dignity; others go further... considering that its foundation is to promote and **realize** that dignity.

It's because of the above that nowadays the minimum requirement for a political system to be considered democratic is respect for human rights. Some also add (myself included) the respect and promotion of economic and social rights. Not to mention the more recent rise of different ideological sectors such as gender, ethnic and cultural rights, which have ended up being added as requirements for a democratic political system. And what about those who consider that a democratic political system needs to promote the viability of the person?, that is, the democracy of creating environments that allow humans to coexist in total harmony with the environment.

Too many conditions, and personally, I'd like all of them to be viable... but they only end up being desirable.

Based on those ideals I mentioned, the defenders of democracy live off shouting these principles to the world over and over in unison, which has brought me to the following question.

> ## And just how democratic is the political system being presented to us?

Taking into account the current situation of my country, the level of those ideals, this question is fairly trivial. However, it's this question that the defenders of democracy regularly tend to ignore. Even the fact of me asking it right now can be seen as a kind of antidemocratic behavior. The discourse about the democratic ideal is so embedded in our roots, it's repeated as much as dembow by politicians and the media, that it seems to have the magical effect of blocking any inquiry into its actual reality. Saying out loud that the prevailing democracy isn't democratic would seem like a terrorist attack against its own stability.

And if reality not only doesn't square with the ideal that's preached about it, but is also so far removed from us that it even flatly contradicts it... maybe I should ask myself the stability of what, exactly, my questions are aimed against.

If we consider those ideals of democracy, it's more than obvious that you can't call democracy systems where private or state monopoly over the media is law; you can't call it democracy where there's a difference in individual capacities to access information and to spread ideas.

It's more than obvious that an institutional framework is not a democratic system when representation is seriously distorted by electoral mechanisms of debatable quality, by the dominance of big corporations over _the representatives,_ the nonexistence of transparency about the acts of each body of the State... the lack of methods of consultation on the problems we have to face and, without going too far, of mechanisms that allow us to immediately and directly remove those authorities of highly questionable professional and ethical standing.

Just as more than 60 years ago our problem was centered on the dictatorship, today it's more than clear that **_our great problem arose out of the regime of "democracy" that followed it._** Dictatorship, democracy, it's more of the same. The dictatorship was the disguise used for a predatory and overexploiting model of government. The democracy they sell us is the same thing, only more efficient.

_The magnitude of all this, the damage and the pain is so great, so evident that I shouldn't doubt what resounds in my mind over and over._

> ## _I don't live in a democratic system. That doesn't exist, it never existed._
